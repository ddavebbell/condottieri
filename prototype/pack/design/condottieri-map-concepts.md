# Map concepts

Twenty-four of them, plus the layout devices underneath. Everything here is
buildable with what the engine already has: field, forest, river, ford, bridge,
rock, marble, void for shape, walls on tile borders with gates, three objective
types, timed and death-triggered waves, and three enemy orders.

The test for every concept is the same: **does the shape ask a question that no
other map asks?** A different backdrop on the same open field is not a new map.

---

## Layout devices

These are the things that actually make maps feel different. The settings are
dressing on top.

| Device | What it does to play |
|---|---|
| **Split start** | Your company begins in two halves that cannot immediately join. Anchoring becomes a race, not a given. |
| **Surrounded start** | You begin in the middle and they are all around you. Every direction is a decision on turn one. |
| **The long room** | A corridor of walls. Nothing flanks; the order of your column is the whole puzzle. |
| **The ring** | Void or wall in the centre, play around the outside. Two routes to everywhere, and they are always a different length. |
| **The island** | Objective across water. Foot wades and is slow; the horse cannot come at all. |
| **The comb** | Parallel walls making three or four lanes. You commit to a lane early and cannot easily change. |
| **The diagonal** | A river or wall cutting corner to corner. Every distance is wrong-footed. |
| **Rooms** | Several small walled chambers with gates. The map becomes four small maps in sequence. |
| **The keyhole** | A wide open field narrowing to a single gate. Their whole force funnels; so does yours. |
| **Scattered cover** | Rocks and copses dotted about. Nothing is a wall, but nothing is a clean line either. |
| **Two objectives** | Two muster regions at opposite ends. You must split, which is exactly what the discipline punishes. |

## Indoors

### 1. The Cellar
*Cellar of a merchant's house — rough stone, barrels, dark.*

A **rooms** map: four small chambers off a central passage, gates between them.
Clear. Their men are distributed one or two to a room, so each chamber is a
small fight you finish before opening the next door. Bodies stop bolts, so the
lead man through each gate is the one who eats the crossbow.

### 2. The Dining Hall
*A long banqueting room, table down the middle.*

The **long room**. A single rock spine down the centre represents the table, so
the hall is two parallel corridors joined only at the ends. Muster at the far
end. Choose a side and live with it — or split and lose your anchors.

### 3. The Chapel
*Small, marble, pillared.*

**Scattered cover** at its tightest: single rock tiles as pillars in a
symmetrical grid. Nothing blocks much, but every sightline is broken, so the
crossbow is nearly useless and the horse — who leaps rock — is suddenly the best
piece on the board. A map that inverts the usual piece order.

### 4. The Catacombs
*Burial tunnels, narrow, branching.*

A maze of walls one tile wide. Hold a junction for four turns. The horse cannot
be hobbled in a corridor because there is only one leg tile — so cavalry becomes
the threat you cannot screen against, and you must kill it rather than block it.

### 5. The Granary
*Stores, grain sacks, a loft.*

**The keyhole**: a wide floor narrowing to one gate, with everything of theirs
behind it. A pure column-discipline test, and the map where the Balestriere
earns his place if you bring him.

### 6. The Stables
*Stalls in rows, a yard outside.*

The **comb** — four stalls as walled lanes opening onto a yard. Their horses
start in the stalls and wake as you approach. Every lane you enter commits you
to one of them.

### 7. The Kitchen
*Fires, water butts, a flagged floor.*

Small map, high density. Water tiles as butts and a drain channel — mire in the
middle of a room, which nobody expects indoors. Clear, five turns, everything
within reach on turn one.

## The villa and its grounds

### 8. The Formal Garden
*Hedges in symmetry, a fountain at the centre.*

Forest tiles as hedge, laid out as a symmetrical maze. Muster at the fountain.
Sliders stop dead on entering hedge, so the garden punishes exactly the pieces
that are strongest in the open. Footmen come into their own.

### 9. The Loggia
*A covered walk along one side of a courtyard.*

**The ring**: a walled block in the centre with a colonnade around it. Two ways
round, always different lengths, and their patrol walks one of them. Timing,
not force.

### 10. The Vineyard
*Rows of vines on a slope.*

The **comb** in soft form: parallel strips of forest instead of walls. You can
cross a row, but it costs your move, so travelling along a row is free and
crossing rows is expensive. Movement becomes directional without a single new
rule.

### 11. The Olive Grove
*Old trees, irregular.*

**Scattered cover** at its loosest — forest tiles placed irregularly, no pattern
to learn. The map that rewards reading a position rather than recognising one.

### 12. The Well Yard
*A small walled yard, one well, one gate.*

Hold the well four turns with their men coming through a single gate. The
cleanest possible statement of the discipline: anchor beside the well and their
infantry simply cannot move you.

### 13. The Dovecote
*A round tower and the ground around it.*

A **ring** made of void — the tower is unenterable, play is the annulus around
it. Clear. Because the tower blocks all sight and movement, their side of the
ring is genuinely hidden from yours until you come round.

## Country and campaign

### 14. The Watermill
*A mill on a race, wheel turning.*

**Island**: the mill sits on a spit between two channels, reachable by one
bridge and one ford. Muster inside. The horse can take the bridge only, so if
they hold it he is out of the map.

### 15. The Marsh Road
*A causeway through wetland.*

The road is bridge tiles; everything either side is river. Foot may leave the
road and wade, slowly; wheels and hooves may not. Hold the road. The whole map
is one long decision about whether to step off it.

### 16. The Quarry
*Cut stone, terraces, spoil heaps.*

Heavy rock in irregular blocks, making a **diagonal** channel from corner to
corner. Clear. Cavalry leaps the spoil and arrives from impossible angles; it is
the best map in the set for the horse.

### 17. The Ambush at the Ford
*Caught mid-crossing.*

A **split start** — three of your men already in the water, two still on the
bank. Wading men move one square. You begin at a disadvantage you did not
choose, and the first three turns are about reassembling.

### 18. The Drovers' Road
*A sunken lane between banks.*

**Long room** made of rock banks rather than walls, with two gaps. Their force
is ahead of you and a wave comes in behind on turn six, so the lane has to be
cleared before you are caught in it.

### 19. The Hilltop Shrine
*A small shrine on high ground.*

Rocks ringing a plateau with two ramps, and a muster region on top. Until
elevation exists as a rule this is rock-and-gaps, and it still works — two
approaches, both narrow, and their crossbow covering one of them.

### 20. Three Farms
*Three walled steadings on open ground.*

**Two objectives** taken further: three small walled compounds, muster two men in
any two of them. You must split, and splitting costs you the discipline. The
map that makes the player feel the cost of their own best mechanic.

## Town and siege

### 21. The Market Square
*Stalls, a fountain, crowds gone.*

An open square with scattered stall-blocks. **Surrounded start**: you begin at
the fountain in the middle and they come from all four streets. Clear. Turn one
is the hardest turn in the game.

### 22. The Gatehouse
*Between the outer and inner gates.*

Two gates in series with a killing ground between them. Muster beyond the inner
gate. Their crossbow covers the space between, and there is nowhere in it that
is not covered — so you cross it fast and in the right order, or not at all.

### 23. The Harbour Steps
*Quayside, water on one flank.*

A **diagonal** map: the waterline cuts the board corner to corner, so your
company forms up along an edge nobody is used to. Hold the top of the steps.

### 24. The Cloister
*Four walks around a garden square.*

**Ring** plus **rooms**: a square colonnade around a central garden, cells off
the outer walk. Their patrol circles the cloister on a fixed loop, guards sit in
the cells. Infiltrate-flavoured without needing the Infiltrate objective —
muster in the garden while the patrol is on the far side.

## Where to start

If you build five of these, build these five — they cover the widest range of
questions and share almost nothing with each other:

1. **The Market Square** — surrounded start, open field
2. **The Formal Garden** — hedges that stop sliders, symmetrical
3. **The Watermill** — island, water denies the horse
4. **The Cellar** — rooms in sequence, gates and bolts
5. **Three Farms** — forced split, the discipline as a cost

Each one takes a terrain grid and a roster, and the solver grades it in about
ten seconds. If a new map comes back at W\\*=3 with lots of slack, it is a
backdrop rather than a map, and the shape needs more work.
